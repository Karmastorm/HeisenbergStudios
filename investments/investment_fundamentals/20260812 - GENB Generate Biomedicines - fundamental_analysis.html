Definitions — tap to expand

Cash runway — How long a company can keep operating at its current spending rate before it needs to raise more money. The single most important metric for a pre-revenue biotech.

P/S ratio — Price vs. revenue. Used instead of P/E when a company isn't yet profitable — though for a biotech this early, even P/S is a weak signal since revenue is mostly collaboration payments, not product sales.

Going concern warning — An auditor's formal statement that a company may not have enough resources to continue operating without additional financing — a serious flag, though common for pre-IPO biotechs that later raise capital to resolve it, as happened here.

Lockup expiration — The date after an IPO when early investors and insiders are first allowed to sell their shares — often creates selling pressure as a large new supply of shares becomes tradeable at once.

ROE / ROIC — Return on equity / invested capital. Structurally negative for almost any pre-revenue biotech reinvesting all capital into R&D — not a meaningful quality signal at this stage.

Phase 1 / Phase 2 / Phase 3 — Successive stages of human clinical trials, testing safety (Phase 1), then early efficacy (Phase 2), then large-scale efficacy and safety (Phase 3) — the last step before an FDA approval decision.

13F filing — A quarterly SEC disclosure large institutional investors must file showing their US equity holdings.
