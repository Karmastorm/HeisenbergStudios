ANSYS MERGER DISTORTS RECENT HISTORY
Synopsys closed its ≈$35B acquisition of Ansys on July 17, 2025. Every FY2025–FY2026 trend below blends organic Synopsys with Ansys, and GAAP EPS has swung wildly quarter to quarter (e.g. Q2 FY2026 GAAP EPS was $0.09 vs. non-GAAP $3.35) due to acquisition-related amortization and deal costs. Source: official Synopsys press releases.
LARGE NEW DEBT LOAD FROM THE DEAL
Long-term debt reached ≈$13.46B post-deal, up from near-zero historically; debt-to-equity jumped from ≈0.08–0.11 (FY2022–24) to 0.50 in FY2025 before easing to 0.36 TTM. Source: Synopsys FY2025 press release, stockanalysis.com.
FTC-MANDATED DIVESTITURES & A 2025 CHINA EXPORT-CONTROL SHOCK
The FTC required Synopsys to divest its optical/photonic software tools (and Ansys to divest PowerArtist) to Keysight as a condition of clearance (final order Oct 17, 2025). Separately, on May 29, 2025 the US Commerce Dept (BIS) barred EDA sales/support to China, forcing Synopsys to suspend guidance and halt Chinese business — rescinded July 2, 2025, but China was ≈16% of FY2024 revenue and analysts flagged lingering customer-confidence damage. Source: FTC.gov, CNBC, DataCenterDynamics.
SOURCES DISAGREE ON FY2025 GAAP FIGURES
The official Synopsys press release states FY2025 GAAP net income of $1.336B (EPS $8.07) and non-GAAP net income of $2.138B (EPS $12.91); a separate data-aggregator pull showed conflicting numbers for the same period. This report uses the official press-release figures as authoritative — verify against the 10-K before relying on any other source's FY2025 numbers.
ACTIVIST INVESTOR NOW ON THE BOARD
Elliott Management built a multi-billion-dollar stake (disclosed ≈March 2026) and settled with Synopsys in May 2026, placing partner Jesse Cohn on the board effective June 1, 2026 — a sign professional investors see real room for margin/capital-allocation improvement post-Ansys. Source: CNBC, Bloomberg.

MODERATE FUNDAMENTALS
Synopsys's underlying business — a duopoly EDA franchise with historically 15-24% returns on capital — remains structurally strong, but its trailing financials are heavily distorted by the ≈$35 billion Ansys acquisition closed in July 2025, which added debt, compressed GAAP earnings and returns, and coincided with a real 2025 China export-control shock and FTC-mandated divestitures that are still working their way through the numbers.
What works for this stock
✓Revenue has grown steadily in the mid-teens percentage range for four straight fiscal years, and the company holds a near-duopoly position in EDA software (≈38% share alongside Cadence's ≈36%) with ≈80-85% recurring revenue and near-100% customer retention.
✓Despite the debt taken on to fund the Ansys deal, leverage remains low in absolute terms (D/E 0.36 TTM, well below 1) and free cash flow has strengthened materially (TTM $2.63B, up from $1.35B in FY2025).
✓Historical returns on capital were genuinely strong before the merger — ROE and ROIC both ran 15-24% in FY2021-FY2024 — suggesting the current TTM weakness (ROE 2.63%) is largely a GAAP/accounting artifact of the acquisition rather than a decline in the underlying business's earning power.
What to watch
⚠The Ansys acquisition has made nearly every trailing financial series (P/E, ROE, ROIC, net margin) temporarily unreliable or misleading due to amortization and deal-cost accounting, and sources disagree on FY2025's exact GAAP net income and EPS figures — worth independently verifying against the 10-K rather than taking any single source at face value.
⚠Synopsys already experienced one real China export-control shock in 2025 (a five-week ban on EDA sales/support that forced a guidance suspension, since rescinded), China remains a meaningful revenue exposure, and an activist investor (Elliott Management) now sits on the board pushing for margin improvement — all signal real, unresolved uncertainty rather than a smooth integration.
Track this going forward
→Whether Q3 FY2026 results (due August 26, 2026) and subsequent quarters show the Design IP segment recovering as management guided, and whether GAAP earnings/returns metrics normalize as Ansys amortization and integration costs wash out of the trailing figures.

Definitions — tap to expand

P/E ratio — What you pay per $1 of profit. Lower vs. history and sector = cheaper. Can be badly distorted by one-time items or acquisition accounting, as it currently is here.

P/B ratio — Price vs. what the company actually owns. A low reading can reflect a genuinely cheap stock, or simply a recently enlarged equity base (as here, from stock-funded M&A).

EV/EBITDA — Full business value check including debt. Lower = better value, all else equal.

ROE — For every $100 shareholders invested, how much profit the company made. Above 15% = good.

ROIC / ROCE — How efficiently the whole business uses capital, including borrowed money. Above 15% = healthy.

Free Cash Flow — Cash left after all expenses and investments. Positive and growing = genuinely healthy business.

13F filing — A quarterly SEC disclosure large institutional investors must file showing their US equity holdings.

CAGR — Compound Annual Growth Rate. Not meaningful when the underlying figure includes one-time items or spans a major acquisition.
