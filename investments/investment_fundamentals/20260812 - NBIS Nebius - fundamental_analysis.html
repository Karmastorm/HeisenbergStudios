Definitions — tap to expand

P/E ratio — What you pay per $1 of profit. Not meaningful when that "profit" is driven by a one-off item rather than operations.

P/S ratio — Price vs. revenue. Often used instead of P/E for growth-stage companies that aren't yet profitable.

EV/EBITDA — Full business value check including debt. Can be mechanically extreme (very high or very low) when EBITDA has only just turned positive off a small base.

ROE — For every $100 shareholders invested, how much profit (or loss) the company made. Above 15% = good — but can be distorted by one-off gains, as flagged here.

ROIC — How efficiently the whole business uses capital, including borrowed money. Above 15% = healthy.

Free Cash Flow — Cash left after all expenses and investments. Deeply negative here because the company is spending heavily on datacenter/GPU capex.

Backlog / RPO — Remaining Performance Obligations: contracted future revenue not yet recognized. High backlog signals demand, but doesn't guarantee the cash to fund the capex needed to deliver it.

Multi-class shares — Some share classes (here, Class B and C) carry more votes per share than the publicly traded Class A shares, letting founders retain control even as their economic ownership percentage shifts.

13F filing — A quarterly SEC disclosure large institutional investors must file showing their US equity holdings.
