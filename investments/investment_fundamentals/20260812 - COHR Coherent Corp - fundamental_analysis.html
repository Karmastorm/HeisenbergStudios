MODERATE FUNDAMENTALS
Coherent's fundamentals show a company that spent three fiscal years working through a debt-funded merger integration — with GAAP losses every year from FY2023 through FY2025 — before swinging decisively back to profitability in the three most recently reported quarters, on the back of accelerating AI-datacenter optical transceiver demand, even as the stock now trades at a rich premium to both its sector and its own history.
What works for this stock
✓Three consecutive GAAP-profitable quarters (Q1–Q3 FY2026: $192.9M, $145.1M, $191.4M) — a clear inflection from FY2023–FY2025's merger-integration losses, driven by the Datacenter & Communications segment growing 37% YoY in the most recently reported quarter.
✓Leverage has fallen sharply — debt-to-equity dropped from a post-merger peak of ≈0.90x (FY2023) to 0.31x (TTM) — while liquidity stayed comfortable (current ratio 3.05x TTM) and S&P upgraded Coherent's secured debt rating to 'BB' in April 2026, citing AI-demand strength.
✓Nvidia's March 2026 $2 billion equity investment and multi-year, multi-product supply/purchase commitment through 2030 gives Coherent unusual multi-year demand visibility from the largest buyer in AI infrastructure, and made Nvidia a top-5 shareholder.
What to watch
⚠The stock trades at roughly 144x trailing earnings and well above its own 3-5 year historical average multiples (P/B ≈5.4-6.0x now vs. ≈2.2x historical average; EV/EBITDA 41-65x vs. ≈15.5x historical average) — a rich valuation that leaves little room for error if growth decelerates or margins disappoint.
⚠Coherent's core raw material, indium phosphide, is exposed to Chinese export-control tightening (6-inch wafer prices reportedly up ≈250%), and a separate FCC proposal that could benefit Coherent by restricting Chinese-made transceiver imports remains an unconfirmed draft, not law — both add real near-term uncertainty on top of the pending Q4 FY2026 results due today.

Definitions — tap to expand

P/E ratio — What you pay per $1 of profit. Lower vs. history and sector = cheaper.

P/B ratio — Price vs. what the company actually owns. Below 1 = buying at a discount to assets.

EV/EBITDA — Full business value check including debt. Lower = better value, all else equal.

PEG ratio — P/E divided by expected earnings growth rate. Below 1 suggests a high P/E could still be reasonable if growth delivers.

ROE — For every $100 shareholders invested, how much profit the company made. Above 15% = good.

ROIC / ROCE — How efficiently the whole business uses capital, including borrowed money. Above 15% = healthy.

Free Cash Flow — Cash left after all expenses and investments. Positive and growing = genuinely healthy business.

13F filing — A quarterly SEC disclosure large institutional investors must file showing their US equity holdings.

CAGR — Compound Annual Growth Rate. Not meaningful when the underlying figure swings from positive to negative, or when it spans a major corporate merger.
