Definitions — tap to expand

P/E ratio — What you pay per $1 of profit. Not meaningful when a company has no trailing profit.

P/S ratio — Price vs. revenue. Often used instead of P/E for growth-stage companies that aren't yet profitable.

EV/EBITDA — Full business value check including debt. Lower = better value, all else equal.

ROE — For every $100 shareholders invested, how much profit (or loss) the company made. Above 15% = good.

ROIC / ROCE — How efficiently the whole business uses capital, including borrowed money. Above 15% = healthy.

Free Cash Flow — Cash left after all expenses and investments. Deeply negative here because the company is spending heavily on GPU capex.

Backlog / RPO — Remaining Performance Obligations: contracted future revenue not yet recognized. The single most-watched CoreWeave-specific metric — high backlog signals demand, but doesn't guarantee the cash to fund the capex needed to deliver it.

Dual-class shares — Some shares (here, Class B, held by founders) carry more votes per share than the publicly traded Class A shares, letting founders retain control even as their economic ownership percentage falls.

13F filing — A quarterly SEC disclosure large institutional investors must file showing their US equity holdings.

CAGR — Compound Annual Growth Rate. Not meaningful, or not yet calculable, for a company with under 3 years of public-market history or a negative earnings base.
