MODERATE FUNDAMENTALS
Nokia's trailing financials show a multi-year telecom-equipment turnaround that hasn't yet delivered growth — revenue has shrunk over both the 3- and 5-year window and returns on capital sit near cyclical lows — but the balance sheet is genuinely strong (low debt, consistent free cash flow, a net cash position) and the most recent quarter shows a real, order-book-validated inflection toward AI-datacenter optical and IP networking demand.
What works for this stock
✓The balance sheet is the strongest in this batch of reports: debt-to-equity has fallen steadily to 0.20 (FY2025), interest coverage is comfortably healthy even at the lower of two conflicting estimates (7.0x), and free cash flow has been positive every year for at least five years, leaving Nokia in a net cash position (≈€2.0-2.8B).
✓AI & Cloud segment revenue more than doubled year-over-year to €446M in Q2 2026, with order intake reaching €2.8B in the quarter alone — roughly 6.3 times that quarter's revenue — and management raised FY2026 growth guidance for both Network Infrastructure (to 12-14%) and combined Optical+IP (to 18-20%) on the back of it.
✓Nokia has not cut its dividend since reinstating it in February 2022, maintaining or modestly growing the payout across four consecutive years despite the earnings volatility below, and its patent-licensing business now has over €800M/year contracted through 2030.
What to watch
⚠Revenue has genuinely shrunk over both the 3-year (-5.8%/yr) and 5-year (-1.9%/yr) windows, and return metrics (ROE, ROIC, ROCE) are all well below their FY2022 peak — the "AI supercycle" narrative is real but has not yet shown up in trailing multi-year growth or returns data.
⚠Nokia has already lost two of its three major US carrier customers this decade — Verizon to Samsung (2020) and AT&T to Ericsson (2023) — leaving T-Mobile as its only remaining major US "Big 3" relationship, a reminder that a strong balance sheet doesn't insulate against real competitive losses in its core RAN business.

Definitions — tap to expand

P/E ratio — What you pay per €1/$1 of profit. Can look inflated when trailing earnings are near breakeven, as here.

P/B ratio — Price vs. what the company actually owns. Below 1 = buying at a discount to assets.

EV/EBITDA — Full business value check including debt. Lower = better value, all else equal.

ROE — For every €100/$100 shareholders invested, how much profit the company made. Above 15% = good.

ROIC / ROCE — How efficiently the whole business uses capital, including borrowed money. Above 15% = healthy.

Free Cash Flow — Cash left after all expenses and investments. Positive and growing = genuinely healthy business.

ADR — American Depositary Receipt: a US-listed instrument representing shares of a foreign company (here, 1 ADR = 1 Nokia ordinary share), letting US investors buy it in USD on the NYSE instead of on the home exchange.

13F filing — A quarterly SEC disclosure large US institutional investors must file showing their US-listed equity holdings — doesn't capture non-US institutional ownership.

CAGR — Compound Annual Growth Rate. Not meaningful when the underlying figure includes a one-time item or swings from positive to negative.
