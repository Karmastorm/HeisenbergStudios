Business Description

Amazon.com, Inc., headquartered in Seattle, Washington, operates through three primary reportable segments: North America and International (e-commerce retail, third-party marketplace services, Prime subscription services, and advertising), and Amazon Web Services (AWS, cloud computing infrastructure and platform services). Beyond its origins as an online retailer, Amazon has built one of the world's largest cloud computing businesses (AWS), a major digital advertising platform, and is now positioning itself as a significant AI infrastructure provider — its own AI business and custom AI chips (Trainium and related silicon) division have each surpassed a $25 billion annualized revenue run rate. Amazon also holds a significant investment stake in AI company Anthropic, whose valuation changes can materially affect Amazon's reported GAAP earnings in a given quarter, as seen in Q2 2026.

Industry Overview & Competitive Positioning

Amazon operates at the intersection of e-commerce/retail and cloud/AI infrastructure — two of the largest and fastest-evolving industries covered in this report series. In cloud, AWS remains the market leader, though Microsoft Azure (integrated with Office 365 and enterprise software, creating switching costs) and Google Cloud (see separate GOOG report) are both credible, well-resourced competitors; one source specifically frames Microsoft, aided by its OpenAI partnership, as AWS's "most formidable cross-platform rival." In e-commerce, Amazon competes against Walmart, Target, and a broad set of specialized and international retailers, while its advertising business increasingly competes with Google and Meta (see separate GOOG and META reports) for digital ad spend.

Amazon's moat spans multiple businesses: AWS's scale, service breadth, and marketplace ecosystem make it "the default choice for startups and digital-native enterprises" per one source; Prime's 200 million-plus subscriber base creates recurring revenue and shopping-frequency advantages difficult for traditional retailers to replicate; and Amazon's advertising business benefits from closed-loop attribution unavailable to most competitors. The company's early and substantial investment in custom AI silicon (Trainium) and its stake in Anthropic also position it distinctively within the AI infrastructure race.
