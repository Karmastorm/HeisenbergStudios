Business Description

Dominion Energy, Inc. is a regulated electric and gas utility holding company headquartered in Richmond, Virginia, serving approximately 2.8 million electric customers primarily in Virginia and North Carolina through its Virginia Electric and Power Company (Dominion Energy Virginia) subsidiary. The company's Virginia service territory includes a significant concentration of data centers in Northern Virginia, a region often referred to as the largest data center market in the world, driving substantial incremental electricity demand and capital investment needs.

Key Revenue Drivers

  • Regulated electric utility revenue from Dominion Energy Virginia, driven by customer growth, rate base expansion, and approved rate case outcomes.
  • Substantial and growing electricity demand from data centers concentrated in Northern Virginia.
  • Regulated gas distribution revenue from remaining gas utility operations.
  • Offshore wind and other renewable generation development.

Key Expense Drivers

  • Capital expenditure on grid modernization, generation capacity additions, and infrastructure to serve data center load growth.
  • Fuel costs for natural gas, nuclear, and other generation.
  • Interest expense on debt used to fund the company's substantial capital investment program.
  • Operations and maintenance costs across its Virginia and North Carolina utility operations.

Industry Overview & Competitive Positioning

The U.S. regulated electric utility industry is characterized by stable, rate-regulated returns on capital investment, and Dominion's Virginia service territory is uniquely positioned as the epicenter of U.S. data center electricity demand growth. This creates outsized capital investment needs and revenue growth potential, but also elevated execution and regulatory risk relative to utilities with more modest demand growth profiles.

Investment Risks

Operational

Execution risk in scaling generation and transmission infrastructure fast enough to meet outsized data center demand growth in Northern Virginia, alongside severe weather exposure typical of Mid-Atlantic utilities.

Financial

Large, ongoing capital expenditure requirements tied to data center load growth create sensitivity to interest rates and capital market access; continued equity issuance to fund capex could dilute existing shareholders.

Regulatory

Rate case outcomes and regulatory decisions by the Virginia State Corporation Commission directly determine allowed returns on invested capital, with data-center-related rate design and cost-allocation debates an emerging regulatory risk.

Legal

The company faces ongoing legal and regulatory risk related to environmental compliance, offshore wind project permitting, and infrastructure siting disputes.
