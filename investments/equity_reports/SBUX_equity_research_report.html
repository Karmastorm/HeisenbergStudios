Given this dispersion and the fact that a single strong quarter does not yet confirm a multi-year recovery, we rate SBUX a HOLD: the turnaround signal is real and the China deal simplifies the balance sheet, but the risk/reward is more balanced than the headline EPS beat alone would suggest until several more quarters confirm the trend.

Recent Developments

Starbucks' Q3 FY2026 results (reported around July 29, 2026) showed EPS of $0.85, a 31% beat versus the $0.65 consensus estimate and up sharply from $0.50 in Q3 FY2025 — the highest quarterly EPS print in at least eight quarters. Revenue of $9.32 billion came in 2.2% above the $9.12 billion consensus estimate, though the reported figure was affected by the company's China joint-venture restructuring: in November 2025, Starbucks finalized a deal selling a 60% controlling stake in its China retail business to private equity firm Boyu Capital for approximately $4 billion, while retaining a minority interest and the Starbucks brand license. This deconsolidates China from reported financials going forward, mechanically reducing reported revenue even as underlying same-store performance (particularly in the U.S., the company's largest market) has improved materially. Global comparable-store sales grew 7.9% in the quarter, and management raised full-year guidance following the print, signaling confidence the improvement represents sustainable momentum rather than a one-quarter anomaly.

Business Description

Starbucks Corporation is the world's largest specialty coffee retailer, operating and licensing tens of thousands of stores globally under the Starbucks brand. Historically built on a "third place" positioning between home and work, the company faced several years of declining U.S. traffic, operational complexity from an overextended menu and customization options, and mixed execution before CEO Brian Niccol (formerly of Chipotle) took over and launched the "Back to Starbucks" turnaround plan, emphasizing faster order-to-serve times, a simplified core menu, improved café ambiance, and renewed marketing focus. In late 2025, the company restructured its China operations by selling majority control to private equity firm Boyu Capital, a move intended to accelerate China store growth under a partner with deeper local market expertise while allowing Starbucks to focus capital and management attention on the U.S. turnaround.

Industry Overview & Competitive Positioning

Starbucks competes in the global quick-service restaurant and specialty coffee industry, most directly against McDonald's McCafé (see separate MCD report in this series) and value-oriented chains such as Dunkin', alongside independent and regional coffee shops. The category has been characterized by intense competition for the daily coffee occasion amid a cautious U.S. consumer, making Starbucks' internal execution turnaround — rather than a category-wide tailwind — the primary driver of its recent outperformance.

Competitive Advantage ("Economic Moat")

Starbucks' moat rests on unmatched global brand recognition, a loyalty/rewards program with tens of millions of active members providing recurring engagement and data, and real estate scale that would be extremely costly for a new entrant to replicate. The moat had been eroding due to execution issues (slow service, an overly complex menu) that the current turnaround plan directly targets; early results suggest the moat is being reinforced rather than permanently impaired.

Valuation Conclusion

We rate SBUX a HOLD. The turnaround evidence in Q3 FY2026 (a 31% EPS beat and nearly 8% comparable-sales growth) is genuinely encouraging and the China joint venture simplifies capital allocation, but the unusually wide analyst target dispersion signals the market has not yet reached consensus on durability. We would look for confirmation across at least two to three more quarters, particularly evidence that comparable-sales growth persists as base-period comparisons normalize, before moving to a more constructive rating.
