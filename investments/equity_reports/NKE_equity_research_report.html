Investment Summary

Investment Thesis Nike is in the middle of one of the most closely-watched corporate turnarounds in the entire report series, and the stock's roughly 30-36% decline in 2026 alone (to a 12-year low) reflects genuine, ongoing operational distress rather than a market overreaction to an otherwise-healthy business. CEO Elliott Hill (an insider brought back specifically to lead the reset) is pursuing a multi-pronged recovery: rebuilding wholesale partnerships, refocusing on core sports and running products, cutting back underperforming product lines (including "quietly shuttering" the Nike Well Collective store concept), closing some stores, and reworking its approach to China — a strategy one source compares to similar insider-led overhauls at Target and Starbucks. There are genuine, quantifiable bright spots: performance revenue grew 39% in the most recently cited quarter, running grew roughly 30%, DTC revenue grew 25%, and wholesale revenue grew 4% (with North America the sole growth geography, +3%). But these gains coexist with serious, unresolved problems: NIKE Direct revenue fell 7% (Brand Digital down 12%), Greater China revenue fell 12% (17% currency-neutral) — continuing a multi-quarter decline — and Converse revenue collapsed 32% across all territories. Reported gross margin and EPS figures have also been significantly distorted by a one-time, roughly $986 million benefit from recovered IEEPA tariffs (which alone added approximately 900 basis points to gross margin and pushed one quarter's reported EPS to $0.72 versus an operational EPS of only $0.20) — meaning headline "beats" during this period should be read with real caution. Management itself frames fiscal 2027 as the phase where "middle innings" turn into visible progress, with a gross-margin inflection expected beginning in Q1 FY2027 and earnings expected "flattish" (excluding tariff-recovery benefits) through the first half of the fiscal year. Given the combination of a genuinely uncertain turnaround trajectory, a brand-new CFO still settling in, continued China weakness, and a Street that has become noticeably more cautious in its most recent individual rating actions (several downgrades and target cuts in the weeks preceding this report) even as the headline consensus rating remains "Moderate Buy," this is a HOLD explicitly flagged as an unresolved turnaround story — the upcoming October earnings and November investor day are the two events most likely to clarify whether the recovery is genuinely gaining traction.

Business Description

NIKE, Inc., headquartered in Beaverton, Oregon, is a global leader in the design, development, marketing, and sale of athletic and casual footwear, apparel, equipment, and accessories, operating a portfolio of brands including Nike, Jordan, and Converse, with revenue reported across North America, EMEA, Greater China, and Asia Pacific & Latin America. The company is currently executing a significant, multi-year operational turnaround under CEO Elliott Hill (an insider who returned to lead the reset), which includes rebuilding previously-damaged wholesale retail partnerships (Nike had shifted heavily toward direct-to-consumer in prior years, a strategy now being partially reversed), refocusing product development and marketing on core sports and running categories, reducing exposure to underperforming lifestyle product lines (which combined account for roughly half of total revenue per one source), closing underperforming stores (including the Nike Well Collective concept), and specifically reworking Nike's approach to the Greater China market, where sales have declined for multiple consecutive quarters.

Industry Overview & Competitive Positioning

Nike operates in the highly competitive global athletic footwear and apparel industry, facing intensifying competition in performance sports specifically, alongside broader macro pressure on consumer discretionary spending. The company's historical strategic emphasis on direct-to-consumer channels over the past several years is now being partially reversed under CEO Hill's wholesale-rebuilding strategy — an implicit acknowledgment that the DTC-first approach damaged important retail partner relationships and contributed to the company's current competitive and operational challenges. One source specifically notes American companies broadly, including Nike, are "losing influence in China as geopolitics, domestic competition and a lack of cultural relevance reshape" that market — framing Nike's China weakness as part of a wider trend affecting U.S. brands there, not purely a Nike-specific execution failure.

Earnings Quality Notes

Nike's most recently detailed quarter is a textbook case for why headline EPS and gross-margin "beats" require careful scrutiny: the $986 million one-time IEEPA tariff-recovery benefit added roughly 900 basis points to reported gross margin and pushed reported EPS to $0.72, while the underlying operational EPS (excluding this benefit) was only $0.20, and core gross margin actually contracted 10 basis points year-over-year when the benefit is excluded. Any analysis of Nike's "recovery" that relies on the headline, tariff-benefit-inclusive figures rather than the operational figures will materially overstate the turnaround's actual progress.
