Investment Summary

Investment Thesis Gilead's core HIV franchise — anchored by Biktarvy and the newly launched long-acting PrEP drug Yeztugo (lenacapavir) — is accelerating rather than decelerating, with management raising full-year 2026 HIV growth guidance twice this year (to 9–10% from an original 6–8%) on the back of PrEP sales that doubled year-over-year to a $4 billion annual run rate. The market appears to be anchoring on deeply negative headline GAAP and non-GAAP EPS figures (driven almost entirely by acquired in-process R&D charges from recent deals) rather than the underlying base-business economics, which show high-single-digit product sales growth, 87% product gross margins, and an "excluding acquisition effects" non-GAAP EPS run-rate in the high single digits. The gap between a depressed headline multiple and a growing, high-margin core business is the central mispricing thesis, though it should be weighed against a well-known 2033 U.S. patent cliff for Biktarvy and intensifying competitive and pricing pressure in HIV.

Business Description

Gilead Sciences, headquartered in Foster City, California, is a biopharmaceutical company focused on discovering, developing, and commercializing medicines for life-threatening diseases, primarily in HIV, viral hepatitis (B and C), and oncology, with a smaller presence in inflammation/respiratory disease and other areas. Its HIV franchise — anchored by Biktarvy (treatment) and the Descovy/Yeztugo PrEP (prevention) portfolio — is the company's largest and fastest-growing segment. The Liver Disease franchise includes Epclusa, Vemlidy, and Livdelzi (for primary biliary cholangitis). Gilead's oncology business, built through acquisitions (Kite for CAR-T cell therapies Yescarta/Tecartus; Immunomedics for Trodelvy), targets hematologic and solid tumor cancers. The company also markets Veklury (remdesivir) for COVID-19, a legacy revenue line that is now shrinking.

Key Revenue Drivers

  • Biktarvy volume and pricing in HIV treatment (largest single product, ~$3.8B/quarter)
  • Yeztugo and Descovy PrEP adoption — the fastest-growing part of the HIV franchise, now at a ~$4B annual run rate
  • Trodelvy expansion into new oncology indications (e.g., first-line triple-negative breast cancer)
  • Livdelzi and other liver-disease product uptake

Key Expense Drivers

  • R&D spend (~$1.4B/quarter) supporting an extensive HIV, oncology, and virology pipeline
  • SG&A investment behind product launches, notably Yeztugo (SG&A +12% YoY in Q2 2026)
  • Acquired in-process R&D and integration charges from recent M&A, which are currently the primary driver of negative reported (GAAP/non-GAAP) EPS

Industry Overview & Competitive Positioning

Gilead competes in the large-cap biopharmaceutical industry, with its core exposure concentrated in HIV treatment and prevention, viral hepatitis, and oncology. The HIV treatment/prevention market is large, durable, and characterized by high switching costs once patients are stabilized on a regimen, but it is also increasingly competitive as long-acting injectable and oral options proliferate. U.S. policy dynamics — including Affordable Care Act subsidy changes that Gilead has flagged as a near-term headwind to Biktarvy demand, and ongoing U.S. drug-pricing/IRA negotiation dynamics — are an important industry-level swing factor for the whole sector, not unique to Gilead.

Competitive Advantage ("Economic Moat")

Gilead's moat rests on its entrenched position in HIV treatment and prevention, built over two decades of clinical leadership, physician relationships, and patient switching costs once stabilized on a regimen. Yeztugo's twice-yearly dosing and reported 70%+ six-month persistency give it a meaningful convenience advantage in PrEP versus daily oral alternatives. The company's oncology moat is comparatively newer and less established, built through acquisitions (Kite, Immunomedics) rather than organic leadership.

Earnings Quality Notes

Reported EPS (both GAAP and headline non-GAAP) is currently heavily distorted by acquired in-process R&D and acquisition-integration charges, producing a negative $6.75 non-GAAP diluted EPS in Q2 2026 despite an underlying, acquisition-adjusted figure of approximately $2.27 for the same quarter. Operating margin was reported as negative 94% in Q2 2026 "reflecting acquisitions," versus approximately 49% excluding acquired IP R&D — a very large adjustment that warrants careful scrutiny of what management includes and excludes. Product gross margin, a cleaner measure of core profitability, was a healthy 87% and flat year-over-year. Analysts should track the cadence and size of these acquisition-related charges closely, since they can obscure the underlying earnings trajectory.
