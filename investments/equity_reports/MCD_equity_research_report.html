Business Description

McDonald's Corporation, headquartered in Chicago, Illinois, owns, operates, and franchises restaurants under the McDonald's brand in the United States and internationally, reporting through three segments: U.S., International Operated Markets (company-operated and franchised restaurants in major international markets), and International Developmental Licensed Markets (markets operated primarily by developmental licensees and affiliates, which have historically shown stronger growth than the core U.S. and Operated Markets segments). McDonald's operates the world's largest quick-service restaurant chain by revenue, with a predominantly franchised business model that generates the majority of its profitability from rent and royalty payments rather than direct restaurant operations, providing a relatively capital-light, high-margin revenue base even during periods of same-store-sales softness (evidenced by the company's steady 47% operating margin even amid the Q2 2026 U.S. comp deceleration).

  • Continued global restaurant expansion, though the pace has been tempered by the delayed 50,000-restaurant target (now 2028, from 2027)

Industry Overview & Competitive Positioning

McDonald's operates in the highly competitive global quick-service restaurant (QSR) industry, where value positioning has become an especially significant competitive battleground amid consumer sensitivity to food and household budget pressures. Management's explicit commitment that McDonald's "will not get beaten on value" reflects the intensity of this dynamic, as lower-income and budget-conscious consumers — historically a core McDonald's demographic — have shown increased price sensitivity across the broader restaurant sector.

Competitive Advantage ("Economic Moat")

McDonald's moat rests on unmatched global brand recognition, an enormous and highly franchised restaurant network providing steady royalty/rent-based cash flow even during operating softness (evidenced by the steady 47% operating margin despite the Q2 2026 U.S. slowdown), and significant purchasing/supply-chain scale. The current U.S. execution challenges are explicitly framed by management as fixable operational issues (complicated launches, inconsistent value messaging) rather than a structural erosion of this underlying brand and franchise-economics moat, a distinction worth taking seriously given management's specificity and directness in the Q2 2026 disclosure.
