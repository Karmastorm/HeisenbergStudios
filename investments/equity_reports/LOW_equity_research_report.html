Investment Summary

Investment Thesis Lowe's is executing a credible, evidence-backed pivot toward the higher-margin Pro and online customer, layering the FBM (Foundation Building Materials) and ADG (Artisan Design Group) acquisitions on top of a core DIY retail base that remains under near-term pressure from a "frozen" housing market and cautious discretionary spending. Q2 FY2026 marked a fifth consecutive quarter of both positive comparable sales and an adjusted EPS beat, evidence that the company's execution is outpacing a soft macro backdrop rather than depending on it. Management's decision to guide to the bottom of, rather than cut, its full-year ranges — while still absorbing real margin drag from acquisition-related intangible amortization — reflects conservatism more than deterioration. The read-through is closely parallel to Home Depot (see separate HD report in this series): both retailers are managing the same delayed "big-ticket" remodeling cycle by leaning into Pro-channel M&A (Lowe's FBM/ADG vs. HD's SRS Distribution) to diversify away from a still-sluggish DIY consumer. We rate LOW a BUY on the view that a housing-driven recovery in large discretionary projects is a "when," not "if," catalyst that the current valuation does not fully credit, while the Pro/online build-out provides earnings resilience in the meantime.

Recent Developments

Lowe's Q2 FY2026 results (quarter ended August 1, 2026) showed net earnings of $2.4 billion and diluted EPS of $4.27, roughly flat year-over-year, while adjusted diluted EPS of $4.40 rose 1.6% YoY and beat the $4.22–$4.23 consensus range by approximately 4% — the company's fifth consecutive adjusted EPS beat. Adjusted EPS included a roughly $0.11-per-share benefit from IEEPA tariff refunds. Total sales rose 8.3% YoY to approximately $26.0 billion, aided by the FBM and ADG acquisitions, though this slightly missed the ~$26.16 billion consensus. Comparable sales rose 0.2% — a fifth consecutive positive-comp quarter — even as comparable transactions declined 2.1%, indicating average ticket and Pro-skewed mix are currently doing more work than traffic. Online sales grew 15.7% year-over-year. Gross margin declined roughly 80 basis points YoY on elevated fuel, transportation, and energy costs, and the company excluded $96 million of pre-tax FBM/ADG acquisition-related expenses from its adjusted EPS figure. Nine of thirteen product categories posted positive comparable sales in the quarter. CEO Marvin Ellison characterized underlying demand by noting, "The good news is that we're not seeing these customers trade down" — that is, big-ticket DIY customers are deferring larger projects rather than downgrading to cheaper alternatives, a distinction management views as supportive of an eventual demand snap-back. Heightened competitive pricing actions in July, particularly in seasonal categories, weighed on sales and comps, and the FBM and ADG acquisitions are themselves facing headwinds from a sluggish residential construction market.

Business Description

Lowe's Companies, Inc. is one of the two dominant U.S. home improvement retailers, serving both do-it-yourself (DIY) homeowners and professional contractors (Pro) through physical stores and a growing e-commerce/omnichannel platform (Lowes.com and the Lowe's app). The company sells building materials, appliances, tools, hardware, lawn and garden products, and home décor. Under CEO Marvin Ellison, Lowe's has pursued a "Total Home" strategy emphasizing Pro-customer penetration, private-brand expansion, supply-chain modernization, and digital investment. The company's FY2026 acquisitions of FBM (Foundation Building Materials) and ADG (Artisan Design Group) extend Lowe's into professional-grade building materials distribution and interior design/installation services respectively, mirroring rival Home Depot's 2024 acquisition of SRS Distribution as both companies race to capture wallet share from professional contractors.

Key Revenue Drivers

  • Pro-channel expansion via the FBM and ADG acquisitions, extending Lowe's into professional building-materials distribution and design/installation services
  • Online sales growth (+15.7% YoY in Q2 FY2026), reflecting continued omnichannel investment
  • Comparable-sales stability (fifth consecutive positive-comp quarter) despite declining transaction counts, driven by average-ticket and Pro-mix strength

Key Expense Drivers

  • Elevated fuel, transportation, and energy costs, which pressured gross margin by roughly 80 basis points YoY in Q2 FY2026
  • FBM/ADG acquisition-related integration expenses ($96 million pre-tax in Q2 FY2026) and ongoing intangible amortization (~40 bps margin drag, ~$0.50/share EPS impact guided for FY2026)

Industry Overview & Competitive Positioning

Lowe's competes in the U.S. home improvement retail industry, an effective duopoly with Home Depot (see separate HD report in this series). Both companies are navigating a "frozen" housing market in which existing-home turnover and large discretionary remodeling projects remain depressed, prompting both retailers to pursue Pro-channel acquisitions (Lowe's FBM/ADG vs. HD's SRS Distribution) to diversify revenue away from a soft DIY consumer.
