Investment Summary

Investment Thesis Costco continues to execute at an exceptionally high level on virtually every operating metric that matters for a membership-based retailer: fiscal Q3 2026 (ended May 10, 2026) delivered net sales of $69.15 billion (+11.6% YoY, well ahead of consensus), comparable sales up 9.8% (6.6% excluding gas/FX), digitally-enabled comparable sales up 21.5% (a third consecutive quarter of 20%+ digital growth), membership fee income of $1.37 billion (+10.7% YoY, aided by the 2024 U.S./Canada fee increase), 41.2 million paid Executive memberships (+9.6% YoY), and a U.S./Canada renewal rate of 92.2% — figures that collectively support the well-worn characterization of Costco's membership model as "unbreakable." The core investment debate is not about business quality, which is essentially undisputed across every source reviewed, but about valuation: Costco trades at a trailing P/E in the mid-to-high 40s and a PEG ratio around 4.5–5.8, an unusually rich multiple that "leaves no margin for a soft quarter," as multiple analysts put it, and one that sits well above the stock's own 10-year median P/E of roughly 37. Management itself has flagged live cost pressures (tariffs, resin inflation, memory-chip costs, wage/healthcare inflation, Middle East shipping risk) and has been deliberately compressing "core-on-core" margins by reinvesting in lower prices to widen Costco's value gap versus competitors — a strategy that is working commercially but structurally caps near-term margin expansion. Given the combination of best-in-class, well-documented operational execution and a valuation that provides little cushion for even a modest deceleration, this is a HOLD: existing holders have every reason for conviction in the business, but the risk/reward for new money at the current multiple is more balanced than the operational narrative alone would suggest.

Recent Developments

Costco's most recently reported full quarter, fiscal Q3 2026 (ended May 10, 2026), showed net sales of $69.15 billion (+11.6% YoY) and total revenue (including membership fees) of $70.53 billion, both above Wall Street expectations (analysts had modeled ~$69.8 billion in net sales per one source). Net income was $2.19 billion, or $4.93 per diluted share (+15% YoY from $1.90 billion / $4.28 per share), meeting adjusted EPS expectations. Comparable sales rose 9.8% company-wide (6.6% excluding gasoline-price inflation and foreign exchange), with the U.S. up 9.4% (6.8% adjusted), Canada up 10.7% (6.2% adjusted), and Other International up 11.2% (5.9% adjusted). Digitally-enabled comparable sales grew 21.5% (20.8% adjusted for FX) — the third consecutive quarter of 20%+ digital growth, driven by improvements to the mobile app, personalized product recommendations, and digital membership card functionality. Membership fee income reached $1.37 billion (+10.7% YoY, or +11% per a slightly different source citation), with roughly a quarter of the growth attributable to the 2024 U.S./Canada membership fee increase; paid Executive memberships reached 41.2 million (+9.6% YoY), total paid members reached 82.9 million (+4.1%), and total cardholders reached ~148.5–149 million (+4%). Renewal rates remained very high: 92.2% in the U.S./Canada (up 10 basis points sequentially) and 89.7% worldwide. Pharmacy was a standout ancillary growth driver, benefiting from increased GLP-1 medication demand and "significant market share gains" per management; fresh and nonfoods categories both achieved high-single-digit comparable sales growth. Gross margin compressed modestly (roughly 20–21 basis points YoY), which one detailed source attributed to deliberate price investments (notably in Kirkland Signature items) funded through SG&A leverage and high-margin membership-fee flow-through rather than core merchandise margin expansion — a sign of Costco intentionally trading near-term margin for market share and member value. Costco opened four new warehouses in Q3 2026 (three in the U.S., one in Canada) and operated 931 total warehouses by quarter-end (639 in the U.S. and Puerto Rico, with the remainder spread across Canada, Mexico, Japan, the U.K., Korea, Australia, Taiwan, China, Spain, France, Sweden, Iceland, and New Zealand). More recently, Costco reported August 2026 net sales of $23.70 billion (a monthly sales figure, not a full quarterly report) and announced plans to open 14 new warehouses across the U.S. and Canada. The company also raised its quarterly dividend by approximately 13% in 2026 and, amid an Iran-related conflict driving oil prices higher, capped and raised prices on Kirkland and Mobil 1 motor oil due to supply constraints. Separately, Costco has been reported to be exploring a Medicare Advantage-related product offering and continuing to expand delivery options for items like custom sheet cakes and party platters.

Business Description

Costco Wholesale Corporation, founded in 1983 and headquartered in Issaquah, Washington, operates a global chain of membership-based warehouse clubs offering a limited selection of nationally-branded and private-label (Kirkland Signature) merchandise across a wide range of categories, sold at high volumes with rapid inventory turnover. The company's distinctive business model earns the substantial majority of its operating profit from recurring membership fees rather than merchandise markup, enabling Costco to sell products at near-break-even prices — reinforcing its reputation as a price leader, driving high customer traffic and loyalty, and sustaining renewal rates near 90% globally (and above 92% in its largest U.S./Canada market). As of the most recently reported quarter, Costco operated 931 warehouses across 14 countries, with the large majority (639) in the U.S. and Puerto Rico, and meaningful and often faster-growing international operations in Canada, Mexico, Japan, the U.K., Korea, Australia, Taiwan, China, Spain, France, Sweden, Iceland, and New Zealand. Costco holds an estimated majority share (cited as over 60% by one source) of the domestic U.S. warehouse-club market.

Key Revenue Drivers

  • Comparable sales growth across merchandise categories (fresh foods, nonfoods, pharmacy — the latter significantly boosted by GLP-1 medication demand), consistently running in the high-single to low-double digits
  • Membership fee growth from new member sign-ups, periodic fee increases (the most recent U.S./Canada increase implemented in 2024 continues to contribute to year-over-year growth), and rising Executive membership penetration (Executive members reportedly spend roughly three times more on average than basic members)
  • Digitally-enabled sales growth, which has now exceeded 20% year-over-year for three consecutive quarters, driven by app improvements, personalization, and digital membership functionality
  • New warehouse openings, both domestically and internationally, with international markets (Other International, Canada) frequently posting faster comparable-sales growth than the U.S. in recent periods
  • Ancillary businesses including gasoline (benefiting from elevated fuel prices and record volumes in some periods), pharmacy, and travel/other services

Key Expense Drivers

  • Deliberate price investments (e.g., in Kirkland Signature products) designed to widen Costco's value gap versus competitors, which have modestly compressed core merchandise gross margin even as overall profitability has grown via membership-fee flow-through and SG&A leverage
  • Tariff-related cost pressure, resin inflation, memory-chip cost inflation, and Middle East shipping disruption — all explicitly flagged by management as active 2026 cost headwinds
  • Rising wage and healthcare costs, a general retail-sector cost pressure specifically cited in analyst commentary reviewed
  • Heavy, ongoing capital expenditure on new warehouse construction and distribution infrastructure globally, which suppresses near-term free cash flow even as it supports Costco's long-term growth algorithm

Industry Overview & Competitive Positioning

Costco operates in the mature, highly competitive discount and warehouse retail industry, specifically the membership-club sub-segment alongside Walmart's Sam's Club and BJ's Wholesale Club. One source specifically distinguishes the "pure warehouse club" sub-segment (a combined ~$431 billion in 2024 sales across the four major U.S./North American operators — Costco, Sam's Club, BJ's, and PriceSmart, per that source) as smaller but faster-growing than the broader supercenter/discount retail category dominated by Walmart. Costco's international expansion continues to outpace its U.S. business in comparable-sales growth in several recent periods, reflecting a still-substantial runway for warehouse-club penetration outside its home market, tempered by the operational challenge of adapting Costco's U.S.-honed model to markets with different labor laws and zoning restrictions (specifically flagged as a risk in parts of Europe by one source).

Threat of SubstitutesModerate — traditional supermarkets, other discount retailers, and e-commerce platforms (including Amazon) all compete for a share of household consumer spending, though Costco's membership-fee-funded pricing model creates a distinct value proposition that is difficult for non-membership retailers to fully replicate.
Competitive RivalryHigh but concentrated — Walmart's Sam's Club is the most direct scale competitor, though Costco is repeatedly described across sources as outperforming Sam's Club on renewal rates, Executive-tier penetration, and digital comparable-sales growth; BJ's Wholesale Club is a smaller, more narrowly regional pure-play competitor.

Competitive Advantage ("Economic Moat")

Costco's moat is widely regarded as one of the strongest and most durable in all of retail, built on a self-reinforcing cycle: membership fees fund near-break-even merchandise pricing, which drives high member traffic and loyalty (evidenced by renewal rates consistently near or above 90%), which in turn supports enormous purchasing scale and negotiating leverage with suppliers, further reinforcing Costco's ability to offer low prices. Rising Executive membership penetration (up meaningfully over the past decade per Morningstar, with Executive members spending roughly three times more than basic members) further strengthens this high-margin, highly predictable membership-fee revenue stream. Morningstar and multiple other sources reviewed treat this moat as effectively unassailable in the near-to-medium term, with the primary risks being execution-related (international expansion into markets with different labor/zoning regimes) rather than competitive-displacement risk.

Valuation Conclusion

Costco's premium multiple relative to Walmart and BJ's Wholesale Club is consistently described across sources as commercially justified by superior growth, renewal economics, and membership-tier penetration — and Costco's recent revenue growth (nearly double Walmart's in the comparison cited) lends real support to that view. However, several of the same sources that endorse the premium as "defensible" also explicitly warn that the current multiple "leaves no margin for a soft quarter" and that "any comparable traffic dip below 2% or Executive membership stall could trigger sharp multiple compression." This is a genuinely two-sided setup: the business quality case for Costco is about as strong and well-documented as any company in this report series, but the valuation leaves unusually little room for error, which is the primary reason this report lands on HOLD rather than BUY despite the clearly positive operational trajectory.

Earnings Quality Notes

Costco's Q3 FY2026 results are well-corroborated across multiple independent sources with consistent figures, a positive signal on data reliability. One detailed source specifically flagged that gross margin compressed roughly 20 basis points year-over-year, and that this compression was "entirely offset by sudden and sharp SG&A leverage and a surge in high-margin membership fees" — an important structural observation: Costco's reported net income growth (+15% YoY) is being driven partly by operating leverage and membership-fee flow-through rather than purely by core merchandise margin expansion, and in fact reflects a deliberate strategic choice (price investment in Kirkland Signature and other items) to widen Costco's competitive value gap at the expense of near-term core margin. This is a genuinely different earnings-quality dynamic than a company where margin expansion reflects pure operating improvement — analysts should understand that Costco's current profitability growth is substantially a function of successfully monetizing member loyalty and scale (membership fees, SG&A leverage) rather than pricing power on merchandise itself, which is consistent with, and supportive of, the company's stated long-term strategy but worth distinguishing from a margin story driven by merchandise pricing power.
