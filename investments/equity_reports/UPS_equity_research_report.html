Business Description

United Parcel Service, Inc. provides package delivery and supply chain management services worldwide, operating through U.S. Domestic Package, International Package, and Supply Chain Solutions segments. The company is executing a strategic shift to reduce its historically large but lower-margin residential/e-commerce delivery volume from its largest customer (Amazon) while investing in higher-margin healthcare logistics (including specialized cold-chain and medical device delivery) and business-to-business supply chain solutions.

Key Revenue Drivers

  • U.S. Domestic Package volume and yield (average revenue per package), increasingly weighted toward higher-margin business-to-business and healthcare shipments as lower-margin Amazon volume is intentionally reduced
  • Healthcare logistics expansion, a key strategic growth investment area supported by the $2 billion 2024–2028 capital commitment
  • International Package segment growth, tied to global trade and e-commerce cross-border shipment volumes

Key Expense Drivers

  • Labor costs, including unionized workforce wage agreements (Teamsters), a significant and closely watched cost line
  • Network restructuring and facility investment costs associated with the healthcare/supply-chain expansion and executive/operating-model changes

Competitive Advantage ("Economic Moat")

UPS's moat rests on its extensive, decades-built integrated ground and air delivery network, established enterprise and healthcare logistics customer relationships, and brand trust in time-sensitive and specialized delivery categories. This moat has narrowed somewhat as Amazon has internalized a growing share of its own delivery volume, motivating UPS's strategic pivot toward higher-margin, less commoditized business lines.
