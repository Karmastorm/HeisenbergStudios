Business Description

RTX Corporation, headquartered in Arlington, Virginia, is a multinational aerospace and defense conglomerate providing systems and services to commercial, military, and government customers worldwide. The company operates through three segments: Collins Aerospace (aerospace and defense products and aftermarket services for civil and military aircraft manufacturers, commercial airlines, regional/business/general aviation, and commercial space operations), Pratt & Whitney (aircraft engines for commercial, military, business jet, and general aviation customers, including the geared turbofan engine family widely installed on narrowbody aircraft, plus auxiliary power units and fleet management/aftermarket MRO services), and Raytheon (defensive and offensive threat detection, tracking, and mitigation systems for government and commercial customers, including sensors, mission orchestration, satellite control products, and software — notably Patriot air-defense systems and AMRAAM missiles). RTX was formed in 2020 through a merger of equals between United Technologies Corporation's aerospace subsidiaries and Raytheon Company (with UTC having previously spun off Otis Elevator and Carrier Corporation), and was renamed from Raytheon Technologies Corporation to RTX Corporation in July 2023.

Industry Overview & Competitive Positioning

RTX operates across both the commercial aerospace engine/systems industry and the global defense industry, giving it a somewhat more diversified end-market exposure than pure-play commercial engine makers like GE Aerospace. The commercial aerospace segment benefits from the same multi-year aftermarket demand recovery affecting the broader industry, while the defense segment benefits from sustained increases in U.S. and international (particularly European and NATO-aligned) defense spending amid heightened geopolitical tensions. A defining company-specific dynamic within this broader industry context has been the multi-year remediation of Pratt & Whitney's geared-turbofan powder-metal contamination issue, which grounded a meaningful portion of the PW1100-powered A320neo fleet — RTX's steady, quantifiable progress on this issue (AOG counts down 25% YTD) is a distinct company-specific catalyst distinguishing its recent performance from the broader industry recovery narrative.

Competitive Advantage ("Economic Moat")

RTX's moat is built on its unusually diversified position spanning both commercial aerospace aftermarket services (Collins Aerospace, Pratt & Whitney) and mission-critical defense systems (Raytheon) — a broader diversification than most single-segment aerospace or defense peers. Its record $289 billion backlog, split across both commercial and defense revenue streams, provides multi-year earnings visibility that is difficult for less diversified competitors to match. The improving GTF fleet-management trajectory, once fully resolved, should also remove a multi-year overhang that has historically distinguished RTX unfavorably from peers like GE Aerospace, potentially narrowing any valuation discount tied to that specific issue.
