The core bull case rests on Kratos successfully scaling Valkyrie production (targeting 1.5 planes/month in 2027, reaching 35–40 units annually by 2028) and its hypersonics business (2025 revenue of ~$200 million, tracking toward ~$400 million in 2026 and ≥$700 million in 2027) while achieving the operating leverage management has promised as fixed infrastructure costs are spread across higher production volumes — an execution-dependent thesis that has not yet been proven at scale, making this a high-conviction but genuinely speculative growth position.

Business Description

Kratos Defense & Security Solutions, Inc. provides mission-critical products, services, and technology solutions for United States national security priorities, operating through two segments: Kratos Government Solutions (KGS) — encompassing microwave electronic products, space/satellite/cyber capabilities, and training solutions — and Unmanned Systems (US), covering unmanned aerial, ground, and seaborne vehicles and related command, control, and communications systems. Kratos is the primary unmanned aerial target-drone system provider for the U.S. Air Force, Navy, Army, and several allied defense agencies, and has expanded significantly into higher-growth adjacent areas including tactical/combat drones (notably the XQ-58A Valkyrie unmanned fighter jet), hypersonic systems and related propulsion (solid rocket motors sourced from partners including L3Harris and Northrop Grumman), jet engines (the Spartan turbojet and, via a joint program with GE Aerospace, the GEK800 engine, which recently received U.S. military type designation F143-ZZ-100 and an EMD contract award for the JASSM program), and space/satellite systems including space domain awareness and software-defined command-and-control constellations. Founded in 1994, Kratos has evolved from a narrower training/target-drone-focused business into a diversified, technology-forward defense company positioned across several of the Pentagon's highest-priority modernization areas.

Industry Overview & Competitive Positioning

Kratos operates in the U.S. defense technology and unmanned-systems industry, benefiting from two structural tailwinds cited across sources reviewed: a global shift away from Chinese-backed defense technology toward domestic and allied supply chains, and the Pentagon's broader strategic shift toward unmanned and autonomous systems. The company competes directly with AeroVironment in tactical/loitering munitions and counter-drone systems, and with a broader set of "new space"/defense-tech names (Red Cat Holdings, Ondas, Unusual Machines) in the unmanned-systems category more specifically targeted by the new U.S. drone tariffs. The broader defense-equipment sector experienced a notable 2026 de-rating despite strong backlogs across multiple names (Kratos, AeroVironment, Red Cat all cited as declining materially even as backlogs grew), which one Bernstein analyst attributed to capital rotation and rate-driven discount-rate pressure on long-duration defense contract cash flows rather than fundamental deterioration — a useful sector-wide framing for interpreting Kratos's own stock decline.
