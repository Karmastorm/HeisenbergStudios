Business Description

Sempra is an energy infrastructure holding company operating through three primary segments: Sempra California, comprising San Diego Gas & Electric (SDG&E) and Southern California Gas Company (SoCalGas), regulated electric and gas utilities serving Southern California; Sempra Texas Utilities, which includes a stake in Oncor, a regulated electric transmission and distribution utility serving Texas; and Sempra Infrastructure, which develops and operates LNG export terminals and other energy infrastructure primarily serving international markets.

Key Revenue Drivers

  • Regulated electric and gas utility revenue from SDG&E and SoCalGas in California.
  • Regulated electric transmission and distribution revenue from Oncor in Texas.
  • LNG export and energy infrastructure revenue from Sempra Infrastructure, including long-term offtake agreements.
  • Rate base growth across all three segments supporting steady earnings growth.

Key Expense Drivers

  • Capital expenditure on grid modernization, wildfire mitigation (California), and LNG infrastructure development.
  • Fuel and gas procurement costs for regulated utility operations.
  • Interest expense on debt used to fund the company's substantial capital investment program across all three segments.
  • Operations and maintenance costs across a geographically diverse utility and infrastructure portfolio.

Industry Overview & Competitive Positioning

Sempra operates at the intersection of regulated utility services (stable, rate-regulated returns) and global LNG infrastructure (driven by global energy demand and long-term offtake contracts). This diversification provides both the earnings stability of traditional regulated utilities and exposure to the higher-growth global LNG export market, which benefits from increasing international demand for U.S. natural gas exports.

Investment Risks

Operational

Exposure to California wildfire risk and associated liability and mitigation costs at SDG&E and SoCalGas, as well as execution risk on large LNG infrastructure development projects.

Financial

Large, ongoing capital expenditure requirements across three segments create sensitivity to interest rates and capital market access; the significant FY2025 net income decline warrants monitoring for recurrence.

Regulatory

Rate case outcomes in California and Texas, along with federal LNG export permitting and international trade policy, directly affect earnings across Sempra's three segments.

Legal

The company faces ongoing wildfire-related litigation and liability exposure in California, as well as environmental and permitting litigation risk associated with LNG infrastructure development.
