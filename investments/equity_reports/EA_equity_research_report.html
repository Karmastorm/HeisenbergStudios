How the Deal Unfolded

EA announced on September 29, 2025 that it had agreed to be acquired by a consortium of PIF, Silver Lake, and Affinity Partners in an all-cash transaction valuing the company at approximately $55 billion enterprise value, with public shareholders receiving $210.00 per share — a 25% premium to EA's September 25, 2025 closing price of $168.50. CEO Andrew Wilson framed the deal as recognition of the company's performance under his tenure, during which (per Silver Lake's Egon Durban) EA had doubled revenue, nearly tripled EBITDA, and grown its market cap fivefold. Shareholders overwhelmingly approved the transaction (roughly 99% of votes cast in favor) at a special meeting on December 22, 2025. The deal then proceeded through regulatory review, including CFIUS (Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States) clearance — a notable step given the Saudi PIF's majority role and the foreign-investment-in-U.S.-critical-technology scrutiny CFIUS review typically implies — which was completed by July 30, 2026. EA stock traded up toward the deal price as closing approached (reaching a 52-week high of $205.96 on July 2, 2026, and closing near $209.70 shortly before delisting), consistent with standard merger-arbitrage price convergence. The transaction officially closed on August 4, 2026, EA stock was delisted from NASDAQ, and shareholders were paid the full $210 per share in cash the following day. In an internal note to employees titled "Our Next Chapter," CEO Wilson named Cam Weber (President and Chief Studios Officer) and David Tinson (President and COO) as continuing key leaders, signaling management continuity through the transition to private ownership.

Business Summary (as of last public reporting, fiscal year 2026)

EA reported fiscal year 2026 GAAP net revenue of approximately $7.5 billion and historic net bookings of $8.026 billion (+9% YoY), driven by its portfolio of major franchises including EA Sports FC (formerly FIFA), Madden NFL, Apex Legends, The Sims, Battlefield, and Need for Speed, spanning consoles, PC, and mobile platforms. The company had topped both adjusted EPS and revenue estimates in at least its last three reported quarters as a public company. Ahead of the deal's completion, EA had also announced workforce reductions (approximately 6% of employees) as part of a cost-cutting and real-estate-footprint-paring initiative, separate from the take-private transaction itself.

Industry & Competitive Context (Historical)

EA operated in the global interactive entertainment/video game publishing industry, competing against Activision Blizzard (now part of Microsoft), Take-Two Interactive, Ubisoft, and other major publishers, with particular strength in sports-simulation franchises (EA Sports FC, Madden NFL) that benefit from exclusive licensing relationships with major sports leagues and players' associations. The acquiring consortium's stated rationale specifically cited EA's "premier sports franchise" positioning and potential to "blend physical and digital experiences" — suggesting the new private ownership may pursue deeper integration with PIF's broader sports and entertainment holdings (PIF has extensive investments across global sports, including golf, soccer, and esports).
