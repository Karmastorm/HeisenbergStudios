Business Description

Mondelez International, Inc., headquartered in Chicago, Illinois, is one of the world's largest snack food companies, specializing in the manufacturing, marketing, and distribution of biscuits, chocolate, gum, candy, and baked snacks across more than 150 countries. The company's portfolio includes globally iconic brands such as Oreo (marketed in over 100 countries and used as a platform for extensive line extensions), Milka, and Cadbury, alongside numerous regional snacking brands. Mondelez was spun off from Kraft Foods in 2012 to separate the global snacking business from the North American grocery business (which became Kraft Foods Group, later Kraft Heinz). The company's business model is particularly exposed to cocoa commodity price volatility given its substantial chocolate portfolio, a structural characteristic that has driven meaningful earnings volatility over the past several years and prompted a deliberate strategic pivot toward a "less cocoa-reliant" product mix over time.

Key Revenue Drivers

  • Emerging market growth (India, Mexico, and Brazil specifically cited as strong performers, with China expected to improve), consistently outpacing developed-market growth
  • Oreo and other core snacking-brand innovation and global line extensions (flavors, ice cream, chocolate bar formats), continuing mid-to-high single-digit growth in many emerging markets
  • Pricing actions taken in response to elevated cocoa costs, which management indicates are largely offsetting cost pressure even as consumers continue spending on snacks despite broader economic uncertainty
  • North American channel expansion into currently under-indexed retail channels, identified by CFO Amit Banati as a significant growth opportunity

Key Expense Drivers

  • Cocoa price volatility, the single most significant and well-documented cost factor for Mondelez, though management notes the industry's current 10-month coverage and supply surplus provide more stability than the acute 2024 price spike
  • Incremental costs from the ongoing Middle East conflict, explicitly factored into current guidance as both a top-line and bottom-line headwind
  • An "unprecedented heat wave" in Europe, which temporarily pressured chocolate consumption and required tighter trade-stock management in Q2 2026
  • Continued investment in the "less cocoa-reliant" portfolio strategy (non-chocolate snacks and fillings), a deliberate long-term de-risking initiative that carries its own execution and R&D costs

Industry Overview & Competitive Positioning

Mondelez operates in the global packaged snack food industry, competing against Nestlé, Hershey, PepsiCo's Frito-Lay/Quaker snacking businesses (see separate PEP report in this series), and numerous regional confectionery and biscuit competitors. The industry's defining recent dynamic has been extreme cocoa price volatility (a severe spike in early 2024, followed by improved supply/demand balance more recently), which has disproportionately affected chocolate-heavy portfolios like Mondelez's relative to less cocoa-exposed snack competitors — directly motivating the company's strategic pivot toward a "less cocoa-reliant" mix.
