Business Description

NextEra Energy, Inc. is a Florida-based holding company operating through two principal subsidiaries: Florida Power & Light Company (FPL), one of the largest regulated electric utilities in the U.S. serving over 12 million people in Florida, and NextEra Energy Resources, the world's largest generator of renewable energy from wind and solar, along with battery storage assets, across North America. The combination of a stable, rate-regulated utility base and a large-scale competitive renewable generation and development business differentiates NextEra from traditional regulated-only utility peers.

Key Revenue Drivers

  • Regulated electric utility revenue from Florida Power & Light, driven by customer growth, rate base expansion, and approved rate increases.
  • Renewable energy generation revenue from wind, solar, and battery storage assets at NextEra Energy Resources, including long-term power purchase agreements.
  • Growth in electricity demand from data centers, electrification, and industrial reshoring within FPL's Florida service territory.
  • Development and sale of renewable energy projects to third parties and yieldco vehicles.

Key Expense Drivers

  • Capital expenditure on grid modernization, generation capacity, and renewable project development.
  • Fuel costs for natural gas and other generation at FPL.
  • Interest expense on debt used to fund the company's large capital investment program.
  • Operations and maintenance costs across a growing renewable generation fleet.

Industry Overview & Competitive Positioning

The U.S. regulated electric utility industry is characterized by stable, rate-regulated returns on capital investment, while the renewable energy generation industry is more competitive and driven by project economics, power purchase agreement pricing, and policy support. NextEra's dual exposure to both provides a differentiated growth profile relative to pure regulated utilities, particularly as electricity demand growth accelerates due to data centers and electrification.

Investment Risks

Operational

Exposure to Florida hurricane risk, which can damage utility infrastructure and require substantial storm restoration capital expenditure, as well as execution risk on a large renewable development pipeline.

Financial

Large, ongoing capital expenditure requirements create sensitivity to interest rates and capital market access; NextEra's growth model depends on continued access to reasonably priced debt and equity financing.

Regulatory

Changes to federal renewable energy tax credits and incentives, as well as Florida rate case outcomes, could affect the economics of both the FPL regulated business and the NextEra Energy Resources renewable development pipeline.

Legal

As a large utility and renewable energy developer, the company faces litigation and permitting risk related to project siting, environmental review, and grid interconnection disputes.
