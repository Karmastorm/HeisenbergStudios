Investment Summary

Investment Thesis TJX Companies delivered a Q2 FY2027 print that "well exceeded expectations" (CEO Ernie Herrman's words), with consolidated comparable sales up 4% — above the company's own plan — and pretax profit margin expanding meaningfully even before adjusting for a one-time tariff-refund benefit. The off-price retail model remains structurally advantaged in the current environment: as full-price retailers work through excess and closeout inventory amid soft discretionary spending, TJX's buying organization is able to source branded merchandise at steep discounts and pass compelling value to an increasingly value-seeking consumer. Growth was broad-based across HomeGoods (+7%), TJX Canada (+6%), and TJX International (+7%), while the flagship U.S. Marmaxx (Marshalls/TJ Maxx) division underperformed at +1% comps, a divergence worth monitoring but not yet concerning given Marmaxx's scale and the strength elsewhere in the portfolio. Management's decision to raise full-year guidance and to accelerate long-term store growth to 4% annually starting FY2028 (raising the long-term global store target to roughly 7,500) signals confidence that the current momentum is structural, not a one-quarter anomaly. We rate TJX a BUY on continued off-price share gains, margin resilience, and a shareholder-return program (buybacks plus dividend) that continues at scale.

Recent Developments

TJX's Q2 FY2027 results (reported August 19, 2026) showed revenue of $15.2 billion, up 5% year-over-year, with consolidated comparable sales up 4%, above the company's plan. GAAP diluted EPS rose 24% YoY to $1.36, while adjusted diluted EPS rose 11% YoY to $1.22, excluding a $0.14-per-share tariff-refund benefit that is included in the GAAP figure. GAAP pretax profit margin expanded 1.9 percentage points YoY to 13.3%, while adjusted pretax margin expanded 0.5 points to 11.9%. By segment, comparable sales grew 7% at HomeGoods, 6% at TJX Canada, and 7% at TJX International, while the flagship U.S. Marmaxx division (Marshalls/TJ Maxx) grew a comparatively modest 1%, below internal expectations. CEO Ernie Herrman specifically highlighted strength in HomeGoods, Canada, and International, while noting Marmaxx's underperformance. Management announced plans to accelerate unit growth to approximately 4% annually starting in FY2028, raising the company's long-term global store count target to roughly 7,500 locations. TJX paid $529 million in dividends and repurchased $798 million of stock (5.1 million shares) in the quarter.

Business Description

The TJX Companies is the largest off-price apparel and home fashion retailer in the world, operating under banners including T.J. Maxx, Marshalls, and Sierra in the U.S. (collectively "Marmaxx"), HomeGoods and Homesense (home décor), and international banners including Winners and T.K. Maxx across Canada, Europe, and Australia. The off-price model relies on an opportunistic buying organization that purchases excess, closeout, and canceled-order inventory from brand-name manufacturers and other retailers at a steep discount, then resells it to consumers at 20%–60% below full retail price. This flexible, low-fixed-commitment buying model allows TJX to maintain strong margins and fresh, "treasure hunt" merchandise assortments regardless of the broader retail cycle, and often benefits from full-price retailers' excess inventory during periods of soft consumer spending.

Industry Overview & Competitive Positioning

TJX competes in the global off-price apparel and home retail industry, most directly against Ross Stores and Burlington Stores in the U.S. The off-price channel has benefited structurally in the current environment as full-price retailers carry excess and closeout inventory amid soft discretionary spending, giving off-price buyers like TJX an especially rich sourcing environment.

Competitive Advantage ("Economic Moat")

TJX's moat rests on decades of vendor relationships and buying-team expertise across more than 100 markets globally, enabling it to source inventory other off-price retailers cannot access at comparable scale or price. Its geographic and banner diversification (U.S., Canada, Europe, Australia; apparel and home) also provides resilience that narrower, U.S.-only off-price peers lack.
