Investment Summary

Investment Thesis General Motors delivered a Q2 2026 print strong enough to trigger the company's second full-year guidance raise of the year, driven primarily by a sharp improvement in North America profitability — adjusted EBIT margin in the region expanded 2.5 percentage points year-over-year to 8.6% — alongside narrowing (though still unprofitable) EV losses. Management raised full-year adjusted EBIT guidance to $14–16 billion, adjusted EPS guidance to $12–14, and adjusted automotive free cash flow guidance to $9.5–11.5 billion, signaling confidence that the improvement in core North American truck/SUV profitability and gradually improving EV unit economics (via scale, battery cost reduction, and manufacturing efficiency) are durable trends rather than a one-quarter anomaly. Importantly, "lower EV losses" is not yet "EV profitability," and tariff policy remains a genuine swing factor for a company with substantial cross-border manufacturing exposure (Mexico, Canada) — a risk analysts continue to price in via a historically wide target range ($61 to $132). We rate GM a BUY: the combination of a resilient, higher-margin North American ICE/truck business funding a gradually improving EV transition, two consecutive guidance raises, and a below-market valuation multiple relative to the raised earnings power supports upside, even as tariff and EV-transition risks argue for a wide margin of safety in position sizing.

Business Description

General Motors Company is one of the largest global automobile manufacturers, producing vehicles under the Chevrolet, GMC, Cadillac, and Buick brands, with a North American business heavily weighted toward high-margin trucks and SUVs. Under CEO Mary Barra, GM has pursued a dual-track strategy: maximizing profitability from its core internal-combustion truck and SUV franchise (its primary profit engine) while investing in an electric vehicle transition (Ultium battery platform, EV manufacturing capacity) that remains loss-making but has shown improving unit economics as scale increases. GM also maintains a financial services arm (GM Financial) supporting vehicle sales financing and leasing.

Key Revenue Drivers

  • North American truck and SUV sales, the company's primary profit engine, where adjusted EBIT margin expanded 2.5 points YoY to 8.6% in Q2 2026
  • Gradually improving EV unit economics as scale, battery cost reduction, and manufacturing efficiency narrow EV losses
  • GM Financial's vehicle financing and leasing operations, supporting overall vehicle sales

Key Expense Drivers

  • Continued EV program investment, which remains loss-making despite narrowing losses in Q2 2026
  • Cross-border manufacturing exposure (Mexico, Canada) that leaves the company exposed to tariff-driven cost increases

Industry Overview & Competitive Positioning

General Motors competes in the global automobile manufacturing industry, most directly against Ford Motor Company given nearly identical North American truck-led profit models and parallel EV-transition challenges, alongside global competitors including Toyota, Stellantis, Tesla, and Chinese EV manufacturers in certain markets.

Competitive Advantage ("Economic Moat")

GM's moat rests on decades of North American truck/SUV brand equity (particularly the Chevrolet Silverado/GMC Sierra franchise), an extensive dealer network, and manufacturing scale. Its EV transition lacks Tesla's first-mover cost advantages but benefits from GM's existing manufacturing footprint and dealer/service infrastructure, which can support EV sales and servicing at a scale newer entrants struggle to match.
