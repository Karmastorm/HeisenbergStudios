Investment Summary

Investment Thesis AT&T continues to execute a disciplined, well-communicated "converged connectivity" strategy — bundling fiber, fixed wireless, and postpaid wireless — that is producing consistently strong subscriber and cash-flow results: Q2 2026 postpaid phone net adds of 432,000 (beating consensus estimates of ~338,500), a record combined 646,000 internet net additions (367,000 fiber, 279,000 fixed wireless), and free cash flow of $4.7 billion, above guidance. The Lumen mass-markets fiber acquisition (closed Q1 2026) is accelerating AT&T's fiber build (2026 targeted as its largest-ever fiber expansion year, reaching 8 million new locations, more than 4 million from Lumen) even though it modestly diluted fiber ARPU in the near term. Management raised its 2026 share-repurchase target to approximately $10 billion (from $8 billion) while reaffirming full-year adjusted EPS guidance of $2.25–2.35 and free cash flow of $18 billion or more, and reiterated a 2026–2028 cumulative shareholder-return commitment of $45 billion or more. The combination of accelerating fiber/wireless convergence, a well-covered ~4.3–4.4% dividend yield, and increasing buybacks (explicitly framed by management as capturing a perceived valuation disparity in the stock) supports a constructive stance, tempered by AT&T's continued exposure to legacy copper-network decline and the general capital intensity of the telecom business.

AT&T reaffirmed full-year guidance: adjusted EPS of $2.25–2.35, free cash flow of $18 billion or more, capital investment of $23–24 billion, low-single-digit consolidated service revenue growth, and 3–4% adjusted EBITDA growth. Business Description

AT&T Inc., headquartered in Dallas, Texas, provides telecommunications and technology services worldwide, operating through two segments: Communications (wireless voice/data services, fiber and fixed-wireless broadband, and business connectivity solutions for U.S. consumer and business customers) and Latin America (operations in Mexico). AT&T's core strategic focus is "convergence" — bundling fiber or fixed-wireless home internet with postpaid wireless service to increase customer retention, average revenue per account, and lifetime value. The company's first-quarter-2026 acquisition of Lumen's mass-markets fiber business significantly accelerated its fiber footprint expansion, and 2026 is targeted internally as AT&T's largest-ever fiber build year.

Key Revenue Drivers

  • Postpaid phone net additions (432,000 in Q2 2026, beating consensus) and low churn (0.86%)
  • Fiber and fixed-wireless internet net additions (a record combined 646,000 in Q2 2026), accelerated by the Lumen acquisition's expanded footprint
  • Rising wireless/broadband "convergence" rate (42–45% of internet customers also taking AT&T wireless), which management links directly to lower churn and higher lifetime value
  • Advanced Connectivity segment revenue growth (guided at 5%+ for 2026, with EBITDA growth of 6%+)

Key Expense Drivers

  • Substantial capital investment ($23–24 billion planned for 2026) to support the largest-ever fiber expansion year (8 million new locations targeted, over 4 million from the Lumen footprint)
  • Legacy copper-network retirement costs, with more than 30% of wire centers targeted for legacy-service discontinuation by late 2026 — a disclosed "tipping point" in this multi-year transition
  • Fiber ARPU dilution from the Lumen-acquired customer base (lower ARPU than AT&T's organic fiber base), though management indicates this is a mix effect rather than pricing weakness in the core business

Industry Overview & Competitive Positioning

AT&T competes in the mature, oligopolistic U.S. telecom industry alongside Verizon and T-Mobile (see separate research reports for both in this series), with an increasingly important "convergence" competitive dynamic across all three major carriers as they each pursue bundled fiber/fixed-wireless-plus-wireless strategies. AT&T's Lumen acquisition specifically accelerates its fiber scale, a key differentiator versus T-Mobile (which relies more heavily on fixed wireless access rather than fiber) and a direct competitive response to Verizon's own Frontier Communications fiber acquisition (see separate VZ research report).

AT&T's moat rests on its large-scale national wireless network, an accelerating fiber footprint (now over 38.6 million locations, targeting 40 million-plus by year-end 2026) significantly boosted by the Lumen acquisition, and a demonstrated ability to convert bundled internet-plus-wireless "convergence" into lower churn and higher customer lifetime value — a strategy management describes as central to its competitive positioning versus Verizon and T-Mobile.
