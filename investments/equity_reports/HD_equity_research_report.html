Business Description

The Home Depot, Inc., headquartered in Atlanta, Georgia, is the largest home improvement retailer in North America, operating 2,364 retail stores and more than 1,340 SRS (professional-contractor-focused distribution) locations across the U.S., Canada, and Mexico. The company sells building materials, home improvement products, lawn and garden products, décor products, and facilities maintenance/repair/operations products, serving both do-it-yourself (DIY) consumers and professional contractors (Pro customers). The SRS Distribution acquisition (completed in 2024) significantly expanded Home Depot's presence in the professional-contractor distribution channel, a business management specifically highlights as comping above the overall company average and driving meaningful market-share gains.

Key Revenue Drivers

  • SRS (professional/Pro distribution) segment growth, comping above company average with positive comps in all verticals — the single strongest-performing part of the business
  • Online/digital comparable sales, up more than 10% YoY for a fourth consecutive quarter
  • International operations (Canada and Mexico), both outcomping the overall company average, with Canada showing positive comps in both transactions and units
  • Average ticket growth ($92.50 from $90.01 YoY) even as transaction counts declined modestly, suggesting customers are spending more per visit on smaller, more affordable projects rather than larger renovations

Key Expense Drivers

  • Tariff-related input costs, substantially offset in 2026 by $730 million in received tariff refunds (with the "vast majority" of expected total refunds now received)
  • Rising transportation, fuel, and energy costs, which management specifically noted reduced Q2 fiscal 2026 EPS by approximately $0.22
  • Continued capital expenditure (~2.5% of sales) supporting new store/SRS location openings (~15 new Home Depot stores, 40–50 new SRS locations planned for the year) and technology/AI investment
  • Net interest expense of approximately $2.3 billion, reflecting the company's leveraged balance sheet (debt-to-equity ~2.64x)

Industry Overview & Competitive Positioning

Home Depot operates in the mature, oligopolistic U.S. home improvement retail industry, competing most directly against Lowe's (see separate LOW report in this series). The industry's central current dynamic is a "frozen" housing market — historically low housing turnover (~3% of housing stock) suppressing large-project/renovation demand even as smaller-project DIY and Pro-contractor spending remains resilient. Both major players in the category have pursued Pro-customer expansion (Home Depot via SRS Distribution) as a growth lever less dependent on the broader housing-turnover cycle.

Continued double-digit digital comp growth for four consecutive quarters also suggests the company's omnichannel/technology investments are translating into durable share gains rather than one-time pandemic-era pull-forward.

Home Depot's Q2 fiscal 2026 results benefited from a disclosed, largely one-time item: $730 million in tariff refunds, of which $685 million was used to directly reduce cost of goods sold — a clean, well-itemized disclosure by management, but one that should not be extrapolated as a recurring gross-margin tailwind at the same magnitude into future quarters (management itself guided Q4 gross margin roughly flat YoY, implying this tailwind normalizes).
